How it works

Tell us your address. We pull your Department of Finance assessment and compare it against recent sales of comparable homes in your neighborhood. If the sales don't support a case, we'll tell you — and there's no charge.

Your application is filed with the NYC Tax Commission in time for the March 15 deadline, and we track it through review. If your assessment is reduced, the saving shows up as a lower bill or a credit — and only then is our fee due.

Why NYC homes get over-assessed

The Department of Finance doesn't visit your home. It values every Class 1 property at once using a statistical model (called CAMA) fed with neighborhood sales data. The model can't see your home's condition, an awkward layout, a noisy block, or a record error — and it prices your home off sales that may not be truly comparable.

Three facts worth knowing:

  • Assessments reset every year. Winning once doesn't protect you next year — and never filing means errors compound quietly.
  • Most owners never appeal. Only a small fraction of the city's 700,000+ Class 1 owners file in a typical year — which is how over-assessments go uncorrected.
  • An appeal asks only for a reduction. The Tax Commission's job is to correct assessments that are excessive, unequal, or unlawful. It reviews the value Finance already set — it cannot change your home's market value.

6% / 20%

The legal caps on how fast a Class 1 assessment can rise: no more than 6% in one year or 20% over five, new construction aside. An appeal is how a starting value that was set too high gets corrected.

Source: NYC Department of Finance, Class 1 Property Tax Guide. The free review shows where your home sits inside these caps.

Questions homeowners ask us

Can filing an appeal raise my taxes?

An application asks the Tax Commission for one thing: a reduction. The Commission's authority is to correct assessments that are excessive, unequal, or unlawful, and it reviews the assessed value the Department of Finance already set. It cannot change your home's market value, property description, or building class.

What does it cost?

The review is free. If we file and your assessment is reduced, our fee is a percentage of your first-year tax saving, set out in a written agreement before we file anything. If there's no reduction, you owe us nothing. One exception: the Tax Commission charges a $175 application fee on properties assessed at $2 million or more — it appears on the tax bill, is never due if review is waived before it's scheduled, and your agreement will state plainly who bears it.

Is my home Tax Class 1?

Almost certainly, if it's a 1–3 family house. Class 1 also covers most residentially zoned vacant land and condominium units in buildings of three stories or fewer. Your tax class is printed on your Notice of Property Value, which the city mails each January — and your free review will confirm it.

What do you need from me?

Very little: your signature on the application, your Notice of Property Value (the city mails it each January), and confirmation of your home's details — rooms, condition, any recent work. We handle the comparable sales, the forms, and the filing.

When is the deadline?

For the 2027/28 tax year, the Tax Commission must receive Class 1 applications by 5:00 PM on March 15, 2027. Mailing it that day is not enough — it has to arrive. There are no extensions. If you miss it, the assessment stands for the full year.

I already pay a tax professional / I appealed before. Does that matter?

No. Assessments are set fresh every year from new sales data, so each year needs its own review. If you filed in a past year, this year's comparable sales may tell a different story.

Do I have to go to a hearing?

No hearing is required. You can ask for your application to be reviewed on the papers alone — in person, phone, or video hearings exist if you or the Commission want one, and your representative appears. That's part of the service.

Are you part of New York City government?

No. We are a private company and are not affiliated with the City of New York, the Department of Finance, or the Tax Commission. You can file an application yourself for free — our service exists for homeowners who would rather have the analysis, paperwork, and deadlines handled for them.
