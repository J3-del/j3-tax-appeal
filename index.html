What an over-assessment costs you

State law caps how fast a Class 1 assessment can rise - 6% in one year, 20% over five. But the caps limit the speed of an increase, not the level. The Department of Finance's own Class 1 guide says it plainly: your assessed value can keep rising even while your home's market value falls.

So if your value was set too high, the extra tax can repeat with each new assessment - and the caps let the assessed value keep climbing toward the model's number. Each year's filing deadline is hard, set by the City Charter. Miss it, and the assessment stands for the year.

$5,000/yr

Say the city is taxing a home as if it were worth $1,200,000, but homes like it are selling for about $800,000. That $400,000 gap is roughly $24,000 of excess assessed value - about $5,000 a year in extra tax at an assumed 20% Class 1 rate. If the gap is still there next year, so is the extra tax.

Hypothetical, at an assumed 20% tax rate, before exemptions and abatements - an illustration of the mechanics, not a prediction for any home. Effective market value = assessed value ÷ 6%, per the Dept. of Finance Class 1 guide.

Why NYC homes get over-assessed

The Department of Finance values every Class 1 home at once, with a statistical model (called CAMA) fed by neighborhood sales and public records. The model can't see a noisy block, an awkward layout, a worn-out kitchen - or an error in its own files. And it prices your home off sales that may not be much like your home.

Three facts worth knowing:

  • Almost no one challenges their assessment. In 2025, Tax Commission applications covered just 4,479 Class 1 tax lots - out of the hundreds of thousands of Class 1 homes across the city.
  • Challenges get a real review - and some win. In 2025 the Tax Commission received 1,767 Class 1 applications and made assessment-reduction offers on 80 of them, per its published annual report.
  • Every year is a new assessment. Finance publishes new values each January. An appeal you don't file by the deadline is gone for that year - the assessment stands.

6% / 20%

The most a Class 1 assessment can rise: 6% in one year, 20% over five. Increases from physical improvements or an expiring exemption don't count against the caps. An appeal is how a value that was set too high gets corrected.

Source: NYC Department of Finance, Class 1 Property Tax Guide. The free review shows where your home sits inside these caps.

Questions homeowners ask us

Can filing an appeal raise my taxes?

An application asks the Tax Commission for one thing: a reduction. The Commission's authority is to correct assessments that are excessive, unequal, or unlawful, and it reviews the assessed value the Department of Finance already set. It cannot change your home's market value, property description, or building class.

What does it cost?

The review is free. If we file and your assessment is reduced, our fee is a percentage of your first year's saving, set out in a written agreement before we file anything. If there's no reduction, you owe us nothing. One exception: the Tax Commission charges a $175 application fee on properties assessed at $2 million or more - it appears on the tax bill, is never due if review is waived before it's scheduled, and your agreement will state plainly who bears it.

Is my home Tax Class 1?

Almost certainly, if it's a house for one, two, or three families. Class 1 also covers most residentially zoned vacant land and condominium units in buildings of three stories or fewer. Your tax class is printed on your Notice of Property Value - and your free review will confirm it.

How do you know whether there's a case?

The same test the Tax Commission applies. We estimate what your home would sell for and compare it with its "effective market value" - the value Finance is effectively taxing you on (your assessed value before exemptions, divided by 6%). If your home is worth less than that number and recent sales back it up, there's a case. If they don't, we tell you that too.

What do you need from me?

About ten minutes with the form below. It asks only what public records can't tell us - things like room counts, alterations, and recent history. Block and lot, property type, ownership, and your assessed value, we look up ourselves from your address. If we find a case, we prepare your application - and nothing is filed until you review and sign it.

When is the deadline?

For the 2027/28 tax year, the Tax Commission must receive Class 1 applications by 5:00 PM on March 15, 2027. Mailing it that day is not enough - it has to arrive. The deadline is set by the City Charter and cannot be waived or extended. (One narrow exception: if the city first notifies you of a new or increased assessment after February 1, you get 20 days from that notice.) If you miss it, the assessment stands for the full year.

When would I see the savings?

If the Tax Commission resolves your application before the city publishes its final assessment roll in May, the property tax bills issued in July for the new tax year can be based on the corrected value - that's the Commission's own description of its calendar in its annual report. Some applications take longer. If there's no reduction, you owe nothing.

I already pay a tax professional / I appealed before. Does that matter?

Usually not. The city publishes new assessments each January, and every year's appeal stands on that year's numbers. If you filed in a past year, this year's comparable sales may tell a different story. One thing to flag: if another representative is already handling this year's appeal for you, let us know before we start, so your filing doesn't conflict with theirs.

Do I have to go to a hearing?

No hearing is required. You can ask for your application to be reviewed on the papers alone - in person, phone, or video hearings exist if you or the Commission want one, and your representative appears. That's part of the service.

How do I know my application was really filed?

You get proof you can hold. The Tax Commission accepts only one proof of timely filing: its own date-stamped receipt, Form TC10 - not a postmark or a mailing receipt. We file in time for the deadline and give you a copy of your stamped receipt.

Who represents me?

A representative named on your application. Tax Commission rules require every representative who files to register with the Commission before the filing season. The Commission sends every notice about your case - including any hearing date - to that representative, and if a hearing is ever scheduled, your representative appears for you. Most Class 1 cases are decided on the papers, with no hearing at all.

Are you part of New York City government?

No. We are a private company and are not affiliated with the City of New York, the Department of Finance, or the Tax Commission.
