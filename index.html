Why NYC homes get over-assessed

The Department of Finance doesn't visit your home. It values every Class 1 property at once using a statistical model (called CAMA) fed with neighborhood sales data. The model can't see your home's condition, an awkward layout, a noisy block, or a record error — and it prices your home off sales that may not be truly comparable.

Three facts worth knowing:

  • Assessments reset every year. Winning once doesn't protect you next year — and never filing means errors compound quietly.
  • Most owners never appeal. In a typical year only a few thousand of 700,000+ Class 1 owners file — which is how over-assessments go uncorrected.
  • Appealing is safe. By law the Tax Commission reviews your application to correct an excessive assessment; it cannot use your filing to raise it.

Questions homeowners ask us

Can filing an appeal raise my taxes?

No. The Tax Commission's authority is to correct assessments that are excessive, unequal, or unlawful. It cannot raise your assessed value on your application. Your filing also doesn't trigger a Department of Finance inspection of your home.

What does it cost?

The review is free. If we file and your assessment is reduced, our fee is a percentage of your first-year tax saving, set out in a written agreement before we file anything. If there's no reduction, you owe us nothing. One exception: the Tax Commission charges a $175 application fee on properties assessed at $2 million or more — it appears on the tax bill, is never due if you withdraw before review is scheduled, and your agreement will state plainly who bears it.

What do you need from me?

Very little: a signed authorization, your Notice of Property Value (the city mails it each January), and confirmation of your home's details — rooms, condition, any recent work. We handle the comparable sales, the forms, and the filing.

When is the deadline?

Class 1 applications must be received by the Tax Commission by March 16, 2027 for the 2027/28 tax year. There are no extensions. If you miss it, the assessment stands for the full year.

I already pay a tax professional / I appealed before. Does that matter?

No. Assessments are set fresh every year from new sales data, so each year needs its own review. If you filed in a past year, this year's comparable sales may tell a different story.

Do I have to go to a hearing?

Usually not. Most Class 1 applications are decided on the papers. If a hearing is scheduled, your representative appears — that's part of the service.

Are you part of New York City government?

No. We are a private company and are not affiliated with the City of New York, the Department of Finance, or the Tax Commission. You can file an application yourself for free — our service exists for homeowners who would rather have the analysis, paperwork, and deadlines handled for them.
